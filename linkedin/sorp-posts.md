## Post 1: Wed 21 Oct, about 8:30am. Image: sorp-1-hook.png

Alt text: Yellow card reading "Your trustees' report now has to show what changed", Charities SORP 2026, Social Intent.

Is your charity ready to show what changed, not just what you did?

The Charities SORP 2026 applies to reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026. For many charities, the biggest shift is in the trustees' annual report.

Tier 1 charities (income up to £500,000) must summarise their main achievements and should consider the difference made. Tier 2 and 3 charities must explain the impact they are making and consider the long-term effect of their work.

A quick test. Score each statement 2 for yes, 1 for partly, 0 for no.

1. Our trustees know which SORP tier our income puts us in.
2. We can show how our activities lead to change.
3. We can say what we achieved in terms of changes for people, not only activity numbers.
4. We collect evidence of outcomes, not only attendance.
5. We know the response rate and limits of our data.
6. Our report says what we cannot yet show.
7. Someone owns the report and starts it early.
8. Our trustees have been briefed on the new requirements.
9. Our impact evidence links to our financial figures.
10. We can describe what volunteers contribute.

14 to 20: you look well prepared.
8 to 13: the foundations are there.
0 to 7: start now, while there is time.

## Post 3: Thu 19 Nov, about 8:30am. Image: sorp-3-story.png

Alt text: Yellow card reading "Strong delivery isn't the same as a strong case", 5 On It Foundation.

Strong delivery isn't the same as a strong case.

5 On It Foundation in Milton Keynes offers free sport, mentoring and creative activity for children and young people facing disadvantage. The delivery is strong and the demand is real. What was missing was the thread from purpose, to programmes, to outcomes, to evidence.

We built a Theory of Change and an impact framework, with the evidence gaps rated red, amber or green so a small team knew where to start.

We were deliberately honest about what is still emerging. A case that overclaims is worse than no case.

That is the standard the SORP 2026 pushes trustees towards: say what you can show, and say what you cannot yet show.
