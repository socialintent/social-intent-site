qa:
  - q: "Should we be a charity or a Community Interest Company?"
    a: "If your purposes are charitable and the tax reliefs would be material, be a charity. A CIC gets no Gift Aid and no mandatory business rates relief, and pays corporation tax. Choose a CIC where the founder must be paid and stay in control, or the activity is mostly commercial, and make that trade-off knowingly."
  - q: "What is the difference between a CIO and a charitable company?"
    a: "A Charitable Incorporated Organisation is regulated by the charity regulator alone, has limited liability and a constitution written for charities. A charitable company limited by guarantee is also registered at Companies House. Larger, contract-heavy or borrowing charities, and those working in more than one UK nation, often prefer the company form."
  - q: "How much can a charity trade before it needs a subsidiary?"
    a: "Trading that advances your purposes, and trading that is ancillary to them, can sit inside the charity. For other trading, HMRC's small-trading exemption allows up to £8,000 of turnover if income is under £32,000, 25% of income between £32,000 and £320,000, and £80,000 above that. The limits apply to turnover, not profit."
  - q: "What does a trading subsidiary actually do?"
    a: "It ring-fences commercial risk so losses stay out of the charity, allows substantial non-primary trading, and passes its taxable profits to the charity under Gift Aid to cut corporation tax. It also gives commercial decisions their own board and accounts. It is not a charity and gets none of the charity reliefs."
  - q: "Can a charity change its structure later?"
    a: "Yes, but at a cost. If UK-wide work is a real plan within five years, build for it now. If it is only a hope, do not shape the structure around it."
